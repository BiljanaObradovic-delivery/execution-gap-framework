# Execution Debt Model™ — Layer Map
*Execution Gap Framework™ · Concept360 · © 2026*

---

Execution debt does not accumulate randomly. Every operational layer has a dominant debt type: the debt that most characterizes the layer when it is underdeveloped or skipped. Where a secondary type is listed, it accumulates alongside the dominant one. Where two types are listed together, both must be addressed before the layer's gate can close.

Each layer closes with a named decision gate. A gate requires documented evidence, not verbal confirmation.

---

## The Eight Execution Debt Types

Technical & Integration Debt · Data & Control Debt · Wallet & Custody Debt · Liquidity & Secondary Market Debt · Operational Resilience Debt · Governance Debt · Vendor Dependency Debt · Compliance & Regulatory Debt

---

## Layer Mapping

| Layer | Dominant Execution Debt | Key Decision Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **L0 — Strategy & Value Definition** | Governance Debt | Investment Approval Gate |
| **L1 — Regulatory & Business Foundation** | Compliance & Regulatory Debt | Regulatory Foundation Gate |
| **L2 — Solution Architecture** | Technical & Integration Debt + Vendor Dependency Debt *(secondary: Wallet & Custody Debt)* | Architecture Approval Gate |
| **L3 — Build & Integration** | Technical & Integration Debt *(secondary: Data & Control Debt; Wallet & Custody Debt)* | Build Acceptance Gate |
| **L4 — Validation & Control** | Data & Control Debt *(secondary: Compliance & Regulatory Debt)* | Operational Readiness Approval Gate |
| **L5 — Readiness & Transition** | Operational Resilience Debt *(secondary: Liquidity & Secondary Market Debt; Wallet & Custody Debt)* | Production Release Approval Gate |
| **L6 — Operational Execution** | Operational Resilience Debt *(secondary: Governance Debt)* | Operational Stability Exit Gate |
| **L7 — Continuous Governance** | Governance Debt | BAU Governance Transition Gate |

---

## How to Read the Map

**Governance Debt opens and closes the program.** It dominates L0 and L7. Ownership that is assumed at the start rather than assigned is the same ownership that is missing when the delivery team exits and operations inherits the program.

**Architecture is where debt becomes expensive.** At L2, technical, integration, and vendor decisions are made together. Custody choices made here lock every downstream workstream.

**Controls must work in practice, not on paper.** L4 is dominated by Data & Control Debt: audit trails, reconciliation, and reporting evidence that were designed too late to be validated independently.

**Go-live is not the finish line.** Operational Resilience Debt dominates both L5 and L6. A program can pass its production release gate and still be unable to operate under real conditions.
